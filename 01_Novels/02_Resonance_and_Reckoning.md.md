# 02. Resonance and Reckoning

The steady thrum of the *Gemos* was suddenly, violently, ripped apart.

It was not the grinding pressure of a gravitational shear or the familiar shudder of a near-miss. It was a sharp, predatory intrusion—the sleek, aggressive silhouette of a Syndicate interceptor flashing across the viewport, its running lights spitting crimson and mockery.

"Contact! Highly irregular signature, non-aligned," Ensign Jax shouted, reacting before the warnings could fully register. "They aren't hailing, Captain. They're hunting."

Vance snapped out of her analysis, the tranquility draining from the bridge like steam from a hot core. The Martian discipline flared, instantaneous and absolute. "Identify target. Status of the captured vessel."

"Pirate vessel, Captain," Victor reported, his posture shifting from skeptical observer to tactical commander. "Lizard-snake morphology. Krym. Black Syndicate signature. They're tracking a high-value cargo runner."

On the tactical display, a tiny, desperate speck—the treasure hunter—was frantically attempting to evade the superior firepower of the Syndicate ship.

"They're after the cargo," Vance stated, her mind already calculating vector and risk. "Target the energy signature. Michael, status of our current FTL stability."

Michael Smith, hunched over his console in the Systems Core Section, felt the familiar spike of adrenaline, a counterpoint to the usual quiet hum of his meticulous work. He watched the telemetry readings with focused intensity, the data flowing past him like a desperate stream. "Stabilizers are holding at eighty-two percent, Captain. The harmonic alignment is... fragile. We have three months of life left in this configuration. Push too hard, and we breach the containment field."

"Fragile is unacceptable," Vance countered, her gaze fixed on the tactical screen. "Victor, maneuvering protocol Delta-Nine. Engaging countermeasures. We are intercepting, not observing."

"Captain, this is a military operation, not a rescue," Victor argued, his voice a deliberate challenge. "We are patrol, not a salvage crew. We maintain distance, we catalog, we report."

Vance did not look at him. Her focus was absolute, fixed on the fragile speck of the treasure hunter. "Victor. We have a third officer who just spent three hours realigning a dying FTL core using nothing but her own biology. We do not abandon an asset in desperate flight."

"Acknowledged, Captain," Victor conceded, the edge leaving his voice, replaced by a grim acceptance of the necessary risk. "Engaging Delta-Nine. Hard course to the prey."
